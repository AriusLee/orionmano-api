# Industry Expert Report — Template (Frost & Sullivan / CIC-style)

**Audience:** Nasdaq IPO underwriters, institutional investors, and the target company's board
**Imprint:** Orionmano Industries (research imprint of Orionmano Assurance)
**Format:** PDF, 25–40 pages
**Purpose:** Independent Industry Expert Report suitable for inclusion (or reference) in an IPO prospectus or transaction deck.

---

## House Rules

1. **Independent voice.** The report is published under Orionmano Industries. It is *not* a client advocacy document — it is a market study that happens to cover the client's industry.
2. **No paid/proprietary data in citations.** Every footnote resolves to a public-domain article on `industries.omassurance.com`. Paid databases (IQVIA, Bloomberg, Refinitiv, etc.) may inform analysis but are never cited directly.
3. **No client-source citations.** Management representations, uploaded client documents, and internal company data never appear as citations. This is an external industry study.
4. **Dual CAGR.** Every market-size figure carries both historical and forecast CAGR, formatted side-by-side.
5. **Exhibit numbering.** Every table and chart is labelled `**Exhibit N: [description]**` and referenced in body text.

### Main Body

#### 1. Sources of Information (Nasdaq prospectus preamble)
This is the first substantive section and mirrors the "Sources of Information" / methodology block that opens the "Industry" chapter of a Nasdaq Form S-1 / F-1 IPO prospectus.

- Statement that industry information is derived from public market research, government publications, trade associations, and Orionmano's own research.
- **Research methodology** — primary research (industry-participant interviews) and secondary research (public company reports, government statistics, trade associations, news, academic research).
- **Base assumptions** — explicit macro assumptions (e.g., "(i) steady GDP growth in the relevant geography; (ii) no material geopolitical disruption affecting trade flows; (iii) continuation of current regulatory regime").
- **Data currency** — as-of date.
- **Limitations** — standard forward-looking-statement caveat.

No `<cite/>` tags in this section — it is methodology, not factual content.

#### 2. Executive Summary
4–6 findings, each anchored to a specific number with a `<cite/>` tag. Cover: headline market size + dual CAGR, key structural driver, competitive-structure observation (consolidation degree + top-N share), one material trend, one challenge, forward outlook. One closing sentence framing the report's scope.

#### 3. Industry Definition and Scope
- Precise scope boundaries — what is IN and OUT of scope.
- Key products/services/categories.
- Primary end-customer segments.
- Relationship to adjacent industries.
- Unit of measurement (retail sales, ex-factory, revenue, GMV, etc.) stated explicitly.

#### 4. Industry Value Chain — Upstream, Midstream, Downstream
Three subsections, one per stage. Each covers: activities, participants, key inputs/outputs, margin profile, concentration. Closing paragraph on where economic value accrues.

#### 5. Market Size and Growth Trajectory
- Opening paragraph with dual-CAGR sentence:
  > The [industry] market was valued at [unit] X in [year], growing at a CAGR of A.B% over [historical window], and is projected to reach [unit] Y by [forecast year], representing a CAGR of C.D% over [forecast window].
- `**Exhibit 1: [Industry] Market Size, [start]–[end]F**` — markdown table with columns: Year | Market Size | YoY Growth. At least 3 historical + 3 forecast years.
- Discussion of inflection points, COVID impact (if relevant), structural shifts.

#### 6. Geographic Market Distribution
- `**Exhibit: Regional Market Split**` — table: Region | Market Size | Share of Global (%) | Historical CAGR | Forecast CAGR.
- Narrative on which regions are gaining share and why.

#### 7. Market Segment Deep Dive
For EACH major segment (3–6 segments): name, definition, latest-year size, dual CAGR, share of total, key sub-segments, publicly observable margin commentary. One `**Exhibit N**` table per segment.

#### 8. Market Growth Drivers and Structural Tailwinds
4–6 drivers. Each: bold name, quantitative anchor (`<cite/>`), mechanism linking to growth. Industry-specific — no "digital transformation" filler.

#### 9. Competitive Landscape

##### 9.1 Player Archetypes and Capability Matrix
- Classify participants into 2–4 archetypes.
- 2–3 representative named companies per archetype.
- `**Exhibit: Capability and Presence Matrix**` — markdown table. Rows = 6–10 named leading players. Columns = capability/presence dimensions specific to the industry. Cells = **Strong** / **Limited** / **Negligible**.

##### 9.2 Financial Benchmarking of Named Peers
- `**Exhibit: Financial Benchmarking of Select Peers**` — markdown table. Rows = 5–8 named public peers. Columns = Revenue | Revenue CAGR | EBITDA Margin | PAT Margin | YoY Growth | ROE/ROCE.
- Include reporting-period and accounting-basis notes under the table.
- 2–3 paragraphs of interpretation: scale leader, margin leader, growth-vs-profitability trade-off.

#### 10. Industry Trends and Evolution
4–6 trends. Each: name, what's happening with quantitative anchor, who is driving, implication.

#### 11. Key Entry Barriers
4–6 barriers ordered by severity. For each: name, mechanism, empirical evidence, how incumbents exploit it. Include capital, regulatory, technology/IP, brand/trust, distribution-access, scale barriers.

#### 12. Market Challenges and Headwinds
4–6 challenges. Name, description, evidence, which cohort is most exposed. Include macro, regulatory, input-cost, demand-side risks. Balanced — not only risks the target company is insulated from.

#### 13. Market Outlook and Future Opportunities
1. Base-case trajectory (restated from Section 5 with `<cite/>`)
2. Upside scenarios (2–3 catalysts)
3. Downside scenarios (2–3 risks)
4. Structural endpoints over 5–10 years

#### 14. Strategic Recommendations
4–6 recommendations. Each: title, rationale (cross-reference earlier sections), applicable cohort, execution considerations. Synthesizes — minimal new external data.

## Citation Format (McKinsey-style footnotes)

Every section that contains factual claims produces a per-section footnote block. Citations render in this exact format:

> Author, "[Title of Article](https://industries.omassurance.com/articles/slug)," Orionmano Industries, Month Year.

Inline: superscript `[^1]` marker immediately after the claim.
Block: horizontal rule + numbered list at the end of the section.

Authors rotate across a fixed analyst roster. Article as-of dates reflect content vintage (e.g., 2023 data → Q1 2024 article date), not the report-generation date.
