# Gap Analysis Framework — Transaction Grade

The Gap Analysis is the **first and most frequently used module** in client engagements. It is the entry point that determines whether a company is ready for a Nasdaq IPO listing, what must change to qualify, and critically — whether the transaction is actually feasible in the real market.

## Purpose

Assess a private company's current state against Nasdaq Capital Market listing requirements and identify specific shortfalls ("gaps") across financial, structural, governance, reporting, legal, and market-readiness dimensions. The output is an actionable, workstream-based roadmap to close each gap before the IPO process begins.

**Key distinction:** This is a transaction-grade advisory memo, not an AI research summary. It must distinguish between:
- **Listing eligibility** — does the company meet the technical Nasdaq quantitative/qualitative requirements?
- **Transaction feasibility** — will underwriters, auditors, and lawyers actually work on this deal? Will institutional investors buy the story?

## When to Use

- **First engagement with a new client** — delivered between first and second client meetings
- **Pre-mandate assessment** — helps both advisory team and client decide whether to proceed
- **Sales process tool** — demonstrates advisory competence and identifies scope of work

## Input Requirements

### Ideal inputs (4 key documents):
1. Corporate proposal / company profile
2. 2 years of audited financial statements
3. Current management reports (latest unaudited financials + KPIs)
4. Shareholder structure, org chart, and cap table

### When inputs are limited:
The system should still generate a useful gap analysis even with only general/public information, but must:
- Clearly flag every data point as "Based on public information" or "Information Required"
- Provide the analytical framework for each section even when data is missing
- Never fabricate or assume specific numbers
- Identify exactly what additional information is needed for a complete analysis

## Assessment Framework

### Phase 0: Listing Path Determination (NEW — MANDATORY)

Before any gap analysis, determine:
1. **Nasdaq tier** — Capital Market / Global Market / Global Select
2. **Registration path** — F-1 (FPI) vs S-1 (domestic issuer)
3. **FPI qualification** — ownership test + business contacts test
4. **Listing vehicle** — direct listing of existing entity vs topco restructure
5. **IPO mechanism** — firm commitment / best efforts / direct listing / de-SPAC

This determination affects EVERYTHING downstream:
- FPI can use IFRS (no US GAAP conversion)
- FPI files 20-F/6-K (not 10-K/10-Q/8-K)
- Reg FD does NOT apply to FPIs
- Many governance rules have home country exemptions for FPIs
- Different Nasdaq tiers have different quantitative thresholds

**CRITICAL:** Even if a company meets quantitative standards, Nasdaq retains discretion to impose additional conditions or deny listing based on investor protection or public interest concerns.

### Phase 1: Financial Assessment

Standard Nasdaq requirements comparison + financial bridge analysis.

**Financial Bridge (MANDATORY):**
```
Current equity → +Fundraising → -IPO costs → -Restructuring → +/- Operations → Pro forma equity
```
Compare against threshold. Show the math — never let the reader assume a fundraising round closes the gap without the bridge.

### Phase 2: Structural Assessment

- Entity structure suitability for listing
- Cap table listability (convertibles, voting rights, float feasibility)
- Audit & accounting readiness (PCAOB, revenue recognition, tax)

### Phase 3: Compliance Assessment

- Governance gaps (with specific Nasdaq rule references)
- Reporting & disclosure gaps (FPI-adjusted)
- Legal & regulatory compliance map (industry & jurisdiction specific)

### Phase 4: Market Assessment

- Peer comparables & valuation reality check
- Transaction feasibility & bankability
- Institutional narrative strength

### Phase 5: Workplan

Structured by workstreams with: current state, red flags, actions, owner, effort, priority, timing.

## Data Consistency Rules

**MANDATORY:** Before writing any section, establish canonical numbers:
- ONE shareholders' equity figure used throughout
- ONE exchange rate used throughout
- ONE revenue figure used throughout
- ONE net income/loss figure used throughout
- If source data conflicts, use the most recent audited figure and note discrepancy once

## Citation Policy

Gap analysis reports do NOT use inline numbered citations [1], [2], [3].
Instead, state the basis naturally:
- "Based on FY20XX audited financial statements..."
- "Per management representations..."
- "According to Nasdaq Listing Rule XXXX..."

Source quality for external references:
- **Tier 1 (preferred):** Official Nasdaq/SEC/PCAOB rules, listed peer filings
- **Tier 2 (acceptable):** Recognised industry databases, government publications
- **Tier 3 (avoid):** Blog posts, market articles, generic web sources

## FPI-Specific Adjustments

When the company qualifies as FPI:
- ✅ Can use IFRS as issued by IASB (SEC explicitly allows)
- ✅ Home country practice exemptions for many governance rules
- ✅ Reg FD does NOT apply
- ✅ Files 20-F/6-K, not 10-K/10-Q/8-K
- ✅ Not required to file quarterly 10-Q style reports
- ❌ Audit committee independence requirements still apply
- ❌ Must still meet quantitative listing standards
- ❌ Must still comply with Nasdaq material disclosure rules

## What Gap Analysis Is NOT

- NOT a due diligence report — no independent verification
- NOT an audit — audited financials taken at face value
- NOT comprehensive — focused on Nasdaq listing readiness
- NOT a marketing document — direct, honest assessment for decision-making

## Relationship to Other Modules

- **Precedes** full Due Diligence — findings inform DD scope
- **Informs** Sales Deck and Kick-off Deck — gaps shape the engagement proposal
- **Feeds into** IPO Readiness Checklist
- **Complements** Risk Assessment — gap analysis is forward-looking, risk assessment is backward-looking
